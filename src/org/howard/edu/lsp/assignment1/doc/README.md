### 1. Are late programming assignments accepted?

No. The Course Requirements section states that each student is expected to "submit all
required work as assigned on the due date and time. **No late assignments will be
accepted.**"

### 2. Where will official course announcements be posted?

On **Piazza**. The syllabus states that students are expected to monitor Piazza regularly
for announcements, resources, and assignment updates. Lecture recordings and notes are also
made available there as needed. Piazza apps are available for Android and Apple phones.

### 3. How will examination dates be announced?

Examination dates are intentionally listed as **TBD** in the syllabus to allow flexibility
based on the pace of the class. The mid-term and final examination dates will be
**announced in class and posted on Piazza at least two weeks in advance**. Students are
responsible for attending class, monitoring Piazza, and being aware of all announced
examination dates; failure to attend class or monitor Piazza is not an acceptable reason
for missing an examination.

### 4. Under what circumstances will a makeup examination be considered?

Examinations must be taken at the scheduled date and time. Makeup examinations are granted
**only under extraordinary circumstances beyond the student's control**. A student
requesting one must notify the instructor as soon as reasonably possible and provide
appropriate supporting documentation. The instructor evaluates the documentation and the
circumstances surrounding the absence in deciding whether a makeup will be permitted — it
is not automatic. Approved makeup examinations may be administered at a different date,
time, or format than the original.

### 5. Is AI permitted in this course?

**Conditionally, yes.** The syllabus recognizes generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, GitHub
Copilot, Claude, Gemini) as an integral part of modern software development, but they
"may be used in this course **only when permitted by the instructor or the specific
assignment**."

Unless otherwise stated, the student remains **fully responsible** for the accuracy,
originality, testing, documentation, and understanding of all submitted work, and must be
able to **explain, modify, debug, and defend** any code, design, or written material
submitted — regardless of whether AI tools were used to create it. Misrepresenting
AI-generated work as one's own is listed as an act of scholastic dishonesty subject to
disciplinary action.

### 9. What documentation is required if you are unable to take an examination because of a medical issue or other approved circumstance?

The student must notify the instructor as soon as reasonably possible and provide
**appropriate supporting documentation**. The documentation should **verify that the
student was unable to participate in the examination at the scheduled time**, but it
**need not disclose a diagnosis or other private medical information**.

Importantly, a doctor's note, urgent care receipt, or verification of a medical visit
**does not automatically qualify** a student for a makeup examination. The instructor
evaluates both the documentation and the surrounding circumstances in making the
determination.

### 10. Does scholarship status entitle a student to different grading, deadline, attendance, or course requirements?

**No.** The syllabus sets out a single set of Course Requirements, a single grading
breakdown (Assignments 35%, Mid-term 25%, Final 25%, Project 15%), and a single set of
attendance, deadline, and examination policies that apply to **each student** without
exception. It contains no provision granting different treatment on the basis of
scholarship status, and scholarship status is not among the grounds it recognizes for
adjusting any requirement.

The only accommodations the syllabus does recognize are formal disability accommodations
arranged through the Office of Student Accessibility (accessibility@howard.edu), which must
be requested at the beginning of the semester and cannot be applied retroactively. A
scholarship obligation is therefore a reason to meet the course requirements, not a basis
for altering them.
